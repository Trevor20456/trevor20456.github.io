Standard 4.1.b and 4.1.c Artifact

Digital Lesson Accessibility Quick Check

A short collaborative review of one digital lesson, followed by one practical accessibility improvement.

Open the completed Quick Check (PDF)Download the completed Quick Check (Word)

Completed Review

Review date: September 3, 2026. Grade/course: Grades 9–12 ELA. Educator role: Secondary English teacher. Material: Google Slides lesson.

Purpose: Review one existing digital lesson or resource with a colleague, identify one practical improvement, and make learning easier to access. This is a supportive conversation, not an evaluation or compliance score.

Agreed change: Replace image-only directions with selectable text, numbered steps, and a brief example.

Why it matters: Students can enlarge, select, or use text-to-speech with the directions and follow the steps more easily.

Implementation and Evidence Record

Implementation: During Week 2, I met with a secondary English teacher for about 15 minutes and used the checklist to review a Google Slides lesson.

Change made: The educator replaced image-only directions with selectable text, numbered steps, and a short example.

Effect on the educator: The educator recognized that image-based text can block text selection and read-aloud tools and planned to reuse the check.

Evidence recorded: Completed checklist, revised slide, before-and-after description, and colleague feedback.

Educator Feedback

What part of the checklist was most useful or made you notice something new?
The selectable-text check revealed that directions inside an image could not be read aloud.
What change will you make, or what did you change, because of this review?
Replaced image-only directions with selectable text, numbered steps, and an example.
Would this tool be useful again? Why or why not?
Yes. The short format fit normal lesson planning.

How It Supports Standard 4.1

4.1.b: The review helps a colleague notice and reduce barriers in digital content and learning tools.

4.1.c: The short, non-evaluative conversation supports collaborative learning and improvement.

I did not collect student-performance data, so this record does not claim improved student achievement.
