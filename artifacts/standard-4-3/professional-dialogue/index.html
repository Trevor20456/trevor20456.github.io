Standard 4.3 · Artifact One

Professional Dialogue About Educator Support

Discussion 5.1: Listening, Responding, and Clarifying Purpose

Trevor Arcadipane · September 2026

Source: EDEL 590, Discussion 5.1, September 2026. Discussion points are paraphrased for this portfolio. The TPDP needs analysis provides the context; the professional exchange and my response to others’ perspectives are the evidence examined here for EDEL 594 Standard 3.

The professional exchange

In Discussion 5.1, I shared a short needs-analysis survey focused on formative assessment. A classmate asked whether educators’ answers would influence which tools I selected and whether the opening question would use written responses or multiple-choice options. My instructor also asked about the response format and recognized the value of keeping the survey short.

These questions highlighted something I needed to explain more clearly: asking educators about their practice is useful only when their perspectives inform the support I offer. The discussion moved my reasoning beyond collecting information toward showing how I would listen and make instructional decisions with other educators.

My response and reasoning

Connecting the discussion to collaborative practice
Discussion contributionMy responseWhy it matters for collaboration
A classmate asked whether current assessment practices would guide the choice of tools.I connect tool selection to educators’ familiar practices and instructional needs. Knowing what teachers already use helps me identify a useful starting point for support.Educators contribute expertise about their own classrooms. Their experience should influence the direction of a coaching conversation.
My classmate and instructor asked whether questions would be open-ended or multiple-choice.I favor select-all options with an “Other” response for common practices, combined with brief written responses for context.A manageable format respects teachers’ time while giving them room to describe needs that preset choices may miss.
My instructor recognized the value of a short survey.I retain a focused approach and connect each question to a decision about support instead of adding questions without a clear purpose.Clear purpose makes participation meaningful and helps establish a respectful professional relationship.

Connection to Standard 3

This exchange illustrates my willingness to consider another educator’s perspective and explain how it changes my thinking. Collaborative coaching requires that same openness: I need to understand a teacher’s goals, acknowledge existing strengths, and make room for questions about my suggestions.

The conversation also sharpened the distinction between an assessment practice and the platform used to carry it out. A teacher’s need to identify misconceptions should guide the discussion before I recommend a digital tool. This principle informs the accompanying resource-review guide by placing instructional purpose and educator choice at the center of the process.

Reflection and demonstrated outcome

The demonstrated outcome is clearer reasoning about how to listen and respond when designing educator support. This artifact documents professional dialogue and reflection; it does not establish that I administered the survey, completed a coaching cycle, or improved another teacher’s classroom practice.

I learned that collaboration includes making my reasoning visible enough for others to question it. For my development as a coach, the strongest part of this exchange is the connection between listening and action: educators’ responses must help shape the goals, resources, and form of support we choose together.
