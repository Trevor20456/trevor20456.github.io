Standard 4.3 · Artifact One

Listening to Educators

Educator Needs Survey and Revision Record

Trevor Arcadipane · September 26, 2026

Purpose: Identify educators’ formative-assessment priorities and translate their responses into practical, personalized support. This artifact documents the survey’s design and revision; it does not report staff survey findings.

From feedback to revision

In EDEL 590 Discussion 5.1, I proposed a short survey about formative assessment. A peer asked whether the first question would guide the choice of tools and whether it would be open-ended or multiple-choice. My instructor also asked about the response format. I used those questions to clarify the survey’s format and make the connection between educators’ responses and coaching decisions explicit.

How responses shape support

Use these examples to begin a conversation, then confirm the priority with the educator. A response guides a shared decision rather than automatically assigning a tool.

Using the responses

  1. Summarize the educator’s priority and ask whether I understood it correctly.
  2. Agree on one lesson goal before choosing a tool or example.
  3. Match support to the preferred format and identified access needs.
  4. Set a follow-up to review the task and the educator’s feedback.

Evidence to retain after use: the agreed goal, the educator’s chosen support, the revision made, and a short reflection on what helped. Public portfolio evidence should omit identifying information.

Design synthesis: Brief choices make the survey manageable, while written responses preserve the educator’s perspective. Separating assessment practices from platforms keeps the focus on instructional decisions. Together, the survey and response guide provide a complete starting point for a focused coaching conversation grounded in the educator’s goals.
